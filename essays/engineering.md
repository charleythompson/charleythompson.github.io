## Choosing Software Engineering

I began learning software engineering because of my interest in video game development. I’ve always enjoyed creating challenges and watching the unique ways that people solve them. At the same time, I am choosing to study computer science broadly because I want a strong foundation that can lead to a stable career, while also giving me the skills to pursue game development on the side. In an ideal world, I would pursue game development full-time. Even if it remains a hobby, however, it has a lot of overlap with the skills I’ve been learning for my career.

## Architecture

One part of programming that I have found myself enjoying is structuring a program before all of the little details are finished. I like figuring out what functions, classes, or systems are going to be needed and building the “skeleton” of the program first. In my Iʻa Quest project, which you can read about on my site, I mapped out the individual parts of the game before even beginning implementation. Some functions initially contained only a comment explaining what they eventually needed to do. I found it surprisingly satisfying to organize the program that way and create a roadmap. It was also really helpful for my teammates because it gave them a lot of direction on what still needed to be done.

## What I Want to Improve

Because of that, I would like to get better at software design and architecture. I want to practice figuring out how larger programs can be divided into smaller systems and how to design code that is easier to understand, expand, and maintain. I also want more experience working with larger teams. Organizing my own smaller programs is relatively straightforward, but designing software that other people can understand and contribute to is a much bigger challenge. Communication is such an incredibly important aspect of software engineering, and I want to become great at it.

## The Future

In the future, I hope to become a well-rounded software developer while continuing to pursue my passion for game development in my own time. General computer science lets me learn skills that apply to many different careers, while game development gives me a creative outlet. I think I would be happiest in a role where I can take a complicated idea, organize it into smaller, bite-sized pieces, and help build the structure that makes the rest of the project possible.
